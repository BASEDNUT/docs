# 🏦 Environmental Balance Sheet — RWA

**Status: LIVE** — [orchard.basednut.com/rwa/carbon-debt](https://orchard.basednut.com/rwa/carbon-debt)

Every industrial system leaves a mark. This ledger places one measurable mark beside one disclosed response: operational emissions, translated at a fixed carbon-market benchmark, and capital directed toward lower-carbon infrastructure.

## What This Measures

> 🌱 pressure → capital → outcome

A transparent two-sided model: 42 companies' disclosed emissions priced at a reference carbon price, against their disclosed environmental reinvestment.

| Column | Definition |
|---|---|
| Modeled burden / year | Operational-emissions tonnes multiplied by the fixed $88/t benchmark. It is a comparison value, not a damage estimate or bill |
| Response investment / year | ACTUAL annual low-carbon capex — company-disclosed spending or commitment for low-carbon transition, prevention, abatement, restoration, or related work. Tags distinguish ACTUAL annual figures from BUDGET or MULTI-YEAR statements |
| Disclosure coverage | Companies with comparable annual figures |
| Response comparison | ACTUAL annual figures only; not restitution |
| Unobserved comparison gap | Not a net liability; disclosure is incomplete |
| Benchmark | Fixed reference, not a damage estimate |

Only ACTUAL annual response capital enters aggregates. Missing disclosure can shrink the observable gap; none of these figures is a legal obligation or investment advice.

## 🧭 What These Numbers Mean

Every row is a historical company disclosure, mostly for one fiscal year. The table turns operational emissions into a single comparison value; it does not measure total environmental harm or predict a company's future cost.

**1. Physical emissions.** Operational emissions (Scope 1 + 2) are the company's direct emissions plus purchased-energy emissions. Scope 3 is shown in details where disclosed, but excluded from this model.

**2. One comparison rule.** Tonnes × the fixed benchmark = one comparable number across all rows.

**3. Economic scale.** Revenue and market cap show how large that comparison value is relative to the issuer. Example: ExxonMobil's modeled burden is divided by its reported revenue and market-cap snapshot; neither percentage is a liability.

Modeled burden / revenue shows scale context only — not a liability, margin, or forecast. Modeled burden / market cap provides investor-scale context only — not a valuation target.

## ⚖️ Two Sides, One Question

Intent is abundant. Reinvestment is scarcer. CDP's aggregate data shows the gap between awareness and capital allocation across the whole economy:

- **72%** of companies reporting through CDP disclose reduction initiatives
- **11%** align capex with transition — the financing gap
- **~1 in 9** of studied companies commit to 100% renewables
